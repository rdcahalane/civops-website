Overview / ROI and justification

Return on investment — the case for visibility

Smyrna produces high-volume, multi-model output across two lines. Every minute of unplanned downtime, every repeat failure, and every delayed maintenance response has a measurable cost. The numbers below are illustrative — built from Smyrna’s known production rate (~58 JPH) and publicly available vehicle pricing data. The Phase 2 engagement will replace these estimates with figures grounded in Nissan’s actual downtime history, MTTR, and quality data.

The Opportunity

What each OEE dimension is worth at Nissan Smyrna

Working assumptions (illustrative). Smyrna runs approximately 58 JPH across a 10-hour shift, producing ~580 vehicles per shift. Blended average selling price across the Rogue, Frontier, Pathfinder, and Murano is estimated at ~$35,000. Typical automotive assembly unplanned downtime runs 6–10% of available time. These assumptions drive the estimates below — actual figures will differ and will be refined during the Phase 2 data mapping workstream.
Availability — downtime reduction
~$200K / shift-point

Each percentage point of uptime recovered at Smyrna’s scale represents 5–6 vehicles per shift. At 580 vehicles per shift and ~$35K ASP, that’s roughly $200K in recovered output — every shift the line runs cleaner.

Basis: 58 JPH × 10-hr shift × $35K ASP × 1pt availability ≈ 5.8 vehicles/shift

Performance — JPH improvement
~$350K / JPH point

Closing even a portion of the gap between actual and target JPH compounds across shifts. One additional vehicle per hour sustained across a full 10-hour shift adds 10 vehicles — roughly $350K in additional output per shift.

Basis: 1 JPH × 10-hr shift × $35K ASP = $350K/shift

Quality — first-pass yield
~$175K / yield point

Each rework loop at Smyrna costs time on the line and carries warranty risk downstream. Closing 1 point of first-pass yield eliminates rework on roughly 5–6 vehicles per shift and reduces the downstream warranty exposure that follows quality escapes.

Basis: 1pt FPY at 580 vehicles/shift × rework cost + warranty tail

Maintenance — response time
~$35K / minute per event

At 58 JPH, each minute of unplanned downtime costs roughly one vehicle in lost output. Reducing average response time by even one minute per maintenance event — across the recurring incidents a plant this size sees daily — accumulates rapidly against the annual baseline.

Basis: 1 min × 58 JPH ÷ 60 ≈ 1 vehicle × $35K ASP per event

Business case

Where value is expected to come from

The strongest near-term value case is not abstract AI. It is faster response, fewer repeat failures, cleaner closure data, and reduced friction between operators, maintenance, Control Room, reliability, and engineering.

Downtime recoveryReduce time lost to slow diagnosis, unclear ownership, repeated information hunting, and delayed escalation.
MTTR / response timeGive the technician likely cause, prior incidents, SOPs, parts context, and escalation support from one event record.
Repeat-failure reductionCapture reason, fix, parts, recurrence flag, and evidence so chronic issues are visible and can be addressed structurally.
Data quality for future AIUse CESMII SM Profiles + i3X-inspired data standards so analytics and AI are trained on contextualized events instead of raw system exhaust.
