Overview / Next steps

From a proven trial to a targeted pilot deployment inside Nissan's own system

The live proof answers the architecture question — the pattern works, on Nissan's real Cimplicity export, read by two independent platforms. What's proposed here is Phase 2: the work that turns a proven pattern into something Nissan's own people can run, on Nissan's own floor, with Nissan's own data. Phase 3 is the scale-out that follows once Phase 2 lands.

Read this as a proposal, not a commitment already made. Durations below are planning estimates for sequencing and resourcing conversations — they firm up once scope, systems access, and the pilot area are confirmed with Nissan.
Phase 2 — four workstreams

Connecting nodes on the map, Open the Sandbox, and Drive the first fully functional pilot

Four pieces, three of them sequential-with-overlap and one optional accelerant. (a) gets the right people fluent in the standards and aligned on the interop pattern. (b) gives everyone a place to build and break things without touching production. (c) is the real pilot — live data, real users, real feedback. (d) is a relationship lever Nissan can pull independent of the other three.

aStandards & interop

i3X / UNS Interoperability Kaizen (working name)

The event itself is 2 days — but a kaizen only works if the 2 days are loaded before they start. That prep is the real work: engaging CESMII, defining the one problem worth locking a room over, getting the right roles in that room (not just IT), and building exercises against Nissan's actual Cimplicity/UNS data — not generic slides. Same shape as any good kaizen: clear problem definition, everything staged to charge the hill, lock the team away, and they emerge having made a real leap on interop — not just watched a presentation about it.

~3–4 weeks prep · 2-day event
bEnvironment

Sandbox environment

An isolated, non-production environment for the pattern to be built and broken in without touching the plant floor. The technical build is the fast part; getting it properly provisioned and cleared through Nissan Systems is usually the pacing item. Budget for that review, not just the setup.

~2–4 weeks · gated by Systems approval
cPilot & adoption

Pilot area deployment & user engagement

The sandbox proves it can be built; this proves it holds up with real users on a real area — live source binding, actual maintenance techs and operators using it day to day, and enough cycles to surface what breaks under real use, not demo conditions.

~4–6+ weeks
dOptional accelerant

Bring Paul to Boston

Get Paul in front of LNS directly and, if the timing lines up, speaking at an LNS event. Not a dependency for (a)–(c) — a lever Nissan can pull to build the relationship and visibility faster, on its own clock.

Optional · timing-dependent
Is this basically a kaizen or hackathon?

Yes — and the Brooksource team's job is making sure Nissan doesn't hit a wall in the middle of it

Same underlying model as any good kaizen event: a clearly defined problem, everything the team needs staged in advance, the room locked until it's solved, and a real leap forward when they emerge — not incremental progress, a step-change. CESMII is a resource Brooksource brings into that room; Brooksource is the point of accountability for proper setup, execution, and follow-up so the two days actually land instead of stalling out.

What CESMII brings

Standards depth, on tap

i3X and SM Profiles expertise, credibility with the standards themselves, and a resource Brooksource pulls in for exactly the sessions where that depth matters — not the party running the event.

What Brooksource brings

Setup, execution, and the follow-up that sticks

Defining the problem worth locking a room over, building exercises against Nissan's actual systems, getting the right people in the room, running the two days, and — the part that most kaizen events skip — making sure the leap forward turns into a standardized work flow afterward.

Phase 3 — scale

Take what the pilot proves, roll it wider

Phase 3 takes whatever Phase 2's pilot proves out (or, if learnings are already folded into the pilot itself, takes the pilot's end-state directly) and extends it to more lines and areas. Realistic sequencing wraps out the remainder of 2026 or continues into 2027, depending on how much ground Phase 2 covers and how many areas Nissan wants to bring on.

  • Integrate whatever Phase 2 surfaces that wasn't already resolved mid-pilot.
  • Expand to additional lines/areas beyond the pilot cell.
  • Timeline is a function of Phase 2 scope — not fixed in advance.

What this needs from Nissan to start

  • Confirm the pilot area and the people who'll actually use it day to day.
  • Clear the sandbox environment through Systems early — it's the item most likely to set the pace.
  • Put the right roles in the room for the interop kaizen, not just IT.
  • Decide whether the Boston trip is worth scheduling now or later.
